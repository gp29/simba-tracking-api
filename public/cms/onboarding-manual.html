Onboarding-Manual

POLICY OVERVIEW

The purpose of this policy is to provide BKTrans staff with a framework in regard to the use of courier services. The policy has been developed and reviewed in line with developments within the Information Governance agenda.

1.0 Introduction

1.1

This policy is intended to identify how BKTrans will handle personal and sensitive data that has to be transferred between departments or other organisations, assuming they cannot be taken personally. Under these circumstances an internal or external courier service would be relied upon to deliver the information in a secure and confidential manner.

1.2

The information could be either paper based or on removable electronic media which has been encrypted.

1.3

For the external transferring of medical records there is a contract in existence. For other sensitive data, the use of registered or recorded delivery through the Royal Mail may be considered appropriate.

2.0 Purpose

2.1

To ensure that BKTrans complies with legislation and NHS standards in respect of information security and in particular the requirements of the NHS in respect of securing personal data which has to be transferred between departments, sites or other organisations.

2.2

This policy applies to both internal courier services and external couriers or mail services, which we may use to deliver data to locations outside of the normal NHS routes within BKTrans

3.0 Who this Policy applies to

3.1

The policy applies to any person directly employed by, contracted or volunteering with BKTrans

4.0 Key Principles

4.1

This policy covers all aspects of information being transferred within or out of BKTrans, including, but not limited to:

  • Patient/client/service user information
  • Personnel information
  • Organisational information.

4.2

This policy covers all aspects of moving information, including, but not limited to:

  • Paper based records
  • Electronically held records on moveable media which has been encrypted.

5.0 Legal Considerations

It is intended that BKTrans complies with NHS and legal requirements for the securing of all information in transit from Dudley CCG premises. By doing so, BKTrans seeks to ensure the:

  • Confidential of personal information
  • Integrity of information
  • Availability of information

THE POLICY

6.0 Objectives

  • Ensure staff are aware of the need to determine the confidentiality and security of information in transit and that if sending sensitive information a suitable means of transporting of the information is used, commensurate with the sensitivity of the information
  • Ensure systems are in place to monitor all aspects of the courier system and that these are reflected in the Information Governance Toolkit assessment
  • Ensure training is in place to inform all staff of their responsibilities with regard to information governance

7.0Duties

7.1 Duties within the Organisation

7.1.1

The Chief Accountable Officer is the Accountable Officer for information security and the system of internal controls.

7.2 Responsibilities of other staff

7.2.1

All staff will ensure that they have read this policy and have undertaken the relevant mandatory training in the subject of information governance

7.2.2

In addition,all staff will abide by the policies and the procedures, regarding information, which have been ratified by BKTrans as well as all legislation and law

8.0 Use of Courier Services

8.1 : BKTrans will have an authorised list of trusted and reliable courier services for routine and secure courier transfers.

8.2 : Internal Routine Data Courier Services

8.2.1

For transfer of PCD, sensitive data or general correspondence within Dudley CCG, check that the internal courier service is appropriate. If it is electronic media please refer to the Safe Haven Policy.

8.2.2

If the courier is not recognised as a member of staff ask them for proof of identity.

8.2.3

If the information transferred is personal or sensitive data get the internal courier service to sign a log book which identifies what is being transferred and where it is going to. Provide the courier with a return receipt to be signed by the intended recipient as proof of delivery. The courier should return the receipt to the dispatcher. The dispatcher should then archive this receipt for an appropriate period. There is no requirement to obtain proof of handover for dispatch for general correspondence.

8.2.4

A telephone call should be made from the despatching body to the intended recipient at the receiving body to notify despatch, if it is personal or sensitive manual data that is being transferred. There is no requirement to call the intended recipient for dispatch of general correspondence.

8.2.5

Information for despatch is placed in a sealed envelope, wallet or preferably where available the organisational approved blue bags. Medical records must only be sent in blue bags (refer to the Records Management Policy).

8.2.6

The courier service should obtain a signature from the receiver if the items required proof of delivery. Proof of delivery is required for personal or sensitive information identified to the courier by the dispatcher. There is no requirement to obtain proof of delivery for dispatch of general correspondence.

8.3 : External Electronic Media Secure Courier Process

8.3.1

Authority to use the courier service is obtained from appropriate level of manaement.

8.3.2

Only authorised courier services are used.

8.3.3

A signature sheet is used to capture details of handover/takeover of the data disks. This information is also recorded on the Log book (Appendix 2).

8.3.4

The data file creation is authorised (name/role/date/time).

8.3.5

The data file is created by (name/role/date/time) and is burned to DVD/CD and encrypted in accordance with Department of Health guidelines.

8.3.6

Packaging is checked to ensure it is sufficient to protect the contents from any physical damage likely to arise during transit such as exposure to heat, moisture or electro-magnetic fields.

8.3.7

The identification of courier is checked before handover of media.

8.3.8

The courier collects the encrypted disk and the signature sheet is signed by both parties.

8.3.9

A telephone call to notify despatch is made from the despatching body to a named individual in the receiving organisation. The data disks are couriered directly to the destination.

8.3.10

Nominated staff at the destination receives the disks and sign the signature sheet and the couriers then notify the despatching body and request pass phrase to decrypt the data is forwarded to recipient.

8.3.11

The staff member responsible for dispatching the data then provides half the pass phrase by telephone and emails the other half of the pass phrase to the nominated receiver. No indication of the connection of the pass phrase to the data transferred must be made either in the telephone call or the email.

8.3.12

The recipient decrypts data with the received pass phrase and confirms that the data can be used by the appropriate database applications.

8.3.13

The disks are then given back to the couriers with appropriate signatures and returned for destruction. Please refer to the organisation’s procedure for the ‘Disposal and Destruction of Sensitive Data.

8.4 : External Secure Courier Process

8.4.1

The courier used for the transfer of manual PCD or sensitive information to a third party i.e. solicitors, Government department should be determined at a local level.

8.4.2

Authority to use courier service is obtained from appropriate level of management.

8.4.3

Only authorised courier services used.

8.4.4

A signature sheet is used to capture details of handover/takeover of the data disks. This information is also recorded on the Logbook (Appendix 2).

8.4.5

Packaging is checked to ensure it is sufficient to protect the contents from any physical damage likely to arise during transit such as exposure to heat or moisture.

8.4.6

The identification of the courier is checked before handover of media.

8.4.7

The courier collects the package and the signature sheet is signed by both parties.

8.4.8

A telephone call to notify despatch is to a named individual in the receiving organisation.

8.4.9

The package is couriered directly to the destination.

8.4.10

Nominated staff at the destination receives the disks and sign the signature sheet.

8.4.11

The couriers then notify the despatching body that the package has been delivered.

9.0 Use of Royal Mail Registered and Recorded Delivery Services

9.1

On occasions it will be deemed appropriate to use the Royal Mail registered and recorded delivery services. In particular this will be for sensitive data that is not patient data. However it will be the responsibility of the department manager to determine what the appropriate level of security in transit should be.

10.0 Monitoring Compliance

10.1

Staff are expected to comply with the requirements set out within the Courier Policy and related policies. Compliance will be monitored via the Manager and Information Governance Team reports of spot checks, completion of staff questionnaires, incidents reported, electronic audit trails and submission of the Information Governance Toolkit

10.2

Non adherence to the Courier Policy and related policies will result in local disciplinary policies being implemented.
